⚖ Important Legal Information

Section 75 – Consumer Credit Act 1974

Section 75 can, in certain circumstances, make a credit provider jointly responsible with a supplier for a breach of contract or misrepresentation. It is commonly relevant where there is a qualifying debtor-creditor-supplier arrangement and the cash price of the goods or services falls within the statutory monetary limits.

If the purchase was made using qualifying credit or qualifying linked finance, and there was a breach of contract or misrepresentation by the supplier, a consumer may have a right to pursue the creditor as well as, or instead of, the supplier. This can be particularly important where the supplier has ceased trading, entered insolvency or is otherwise unable to meet a claim.

Misrepresentation Act 1967

A misrepresentation may arise where a false statement of fact or law is made and induces someone to enter into a contract. Depending on the circumstances, remedies may include rescission and/or damages. Statements about expected savings, income, performance, suitability or payback can be relevant if they were false or misleading and materially influenced the decision to buy.

Your answers are used to highlight these potential routes, but the facts, contract, transaction structure and available evidence determine whether a legal claim exists.
